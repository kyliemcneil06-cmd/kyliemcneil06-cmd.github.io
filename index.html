Reading Response: Glitch studies Manifesto

Date: May 13

Glitches are the gateway to ultimate discovery and creation, but because of its almost limitless possibilities it can be destructive overwhelming and infectious Glitches are the unknown, the diversion of the mainstream and accepted

the way that this relates to our class is that we are going to encounter glitches all. the. time, so its a good strategy to find the good in them and potentially utalize them to your advantage

there is art in the act of destruction and dismantling

Glitches can be unpredictable and uncontrollable, sometimes they are spontaneous and sometimes they are slow. It is very hard to control but when it is mastered it can lead to some beautiful creations

My only question is if someone manages to master glitches and manages to create things constantly does that still qualify as a glitch? Or just an art work? Aren't glitches the absence of something working?

This is a bit of a shallow comparison (a little less artistic then the article) but as I read this all I could think about was an accidental glitch in the earlier development stages of Minecraft that resulted in one of if not the most iconic mob in minecraft. The creeper was accidentally created from the glitched out code of a pig. So glitches can create iconic things

Reading Response: THE AESTHETIC OF FAILURE

Date: May 20

I had a bit of a hard time fully comprehending this reading, I’m sorry if I completely misunderstood something.

I think we as a society have exempted net art and digital art to be a true form of art. The only (semi-close comparison I can sort of make to this phenomenon) is AI art, can this new form of digital “creating” be considered art? (THE ANSWER IS NO, but it is still a pretty common debate you see around).

“One thing mechanical reproduction cannot, by definition, reproduce is authenticity.” I very much agree with this specific quote especially with the prevalence of AI as we know

I found it interesting when the reading mentioned how net art isn’t the same as other types of art because it isn’t physical, it can so easily be downloaded, moved around and stolen. While other art pieces are physical and typically(?) not as accessible physically to the general public. Does this constitute denying web art as an art form, I don’t think so.

I think regular art such as painting, sculpture, drawing etc can be just as repetitive as web art

I understand making digital art easier to access for the general public is a good thing, because art isn’t just for the elites (or whichever other small demographic) it's for everyone. But at the same time unfortunately it makes it more available for stealing and such.

I like this recurring concept of mistakes can be interpreted/created into art

“Jodi suggests that some spectators are unwilling to give up certain forms of programming logic and control” we discussed this from last weeks reading, I am very guilty of this, I like this working out making sense, so last project was a wee bit of a challenge for my perfectionist side

Idk how we got from online erotic flickering to pong, but we are here now

Reading Response: Software structures

Date: May 28

Sorry if I misinterpreted anything. I think it's interesting how Lewitt uses people to interpret and program his work. I think it brings a whole new level of artistry to his work. Especially in an age where digital media, digital art forms and AI are all wrapped together in the question what is art? I appreciate his inclusion of people to his process because I think and (I think the article agrees) the more human interpretation, artistry and creativity you add to the pile the more innovation you end up with. I also think it's really impressive he did this with a pre-written code, he managed to give something that is pretty straight forward and set in stone, and make it a collaborative effort with room for creativity.

Reading Response: Introduction to net.art (Shulgin & Bookchin. 1994)

Date: June 2 /p>

This article feels both old and way ahead of its time.

On one hand it feels like it was written in the 2010s because it has that specific “break the rules" (spunky, hopeful?) tone that I associate with the 2010s and on top of that it's one of those: how to achieve success lists. But at the same time it feels like it's from the late 90s because it uses terms like “hits” and internet clients that I personally have never heard of. Also when it describes the internet space it's missing so many large aspects of it that have emerged in recent years.

I like that there are still small aspects of this list’s instructions that still can be applied/helpful today despite being over 27 years old. But at the same time the art landscape of the internet has completely changed, and it's not as simple as following a step by step guide to become successful or even noticed with your internet art, especially with rampant AI and oversaturation

Speaking of AI, the line “Collaboration without consideration of appropriation of ideas” made me cringe just a little, but I guess digital art theft and exploitation wasn’t as prevalent in the late 90s(?)
